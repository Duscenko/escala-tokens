## Design Context

### Users
Design engineers — people who bridge design and code. They're comfortable reading CSS variables, understand Figma token architecture, and know what a semantic token role like `text-secondary_hover` means. They're building or standardizing a design system for a team or product, and they want a reliable, precise tool that respects their expertise. They are not beginners; they don't need hand-holding, but they do expect clarity and density without noise.

### Brand Personality
Professional and precise. Think Linear, Vercel, or Raycast — tools that feel like they were built by someone who cares about craft. The tone is confident, not arrogant. Dense but not cluttered. Trustworthy infrastructure, not a flashy demo. Three words: **Credible. Precise. Quiet.**

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark mode only. Neutral-950 base (`#0a0a0a`). Intentional, not placeholder. The accent color is currently a generic blue — this is open to evolving toward something more distinctive. Typography should feel purposeful and technical. No decorative elements for their own sake. Visual hierarchy through contrast and spacing, not color explosions.

Anti-references: flashy SaaS landing-page aesthetic, gradient-heavy "AI tool" look, rounded-everything friendly UX, neon-on-dark.

### Design Principles
1. **Density with clarity** — Show a lot of information at once, but make it scannable. Design engineers don't need progressive disclosure for basic settings; they do need it for 80-token lists.
2. **Trust the user** — Avoid over-explaining. Labels should be identifiers, not tutorials. When something is obvious from context, skip the description.
3. **Feedback is instant** — Every change produces a visible output (scale swatch, token preview, specimen). Latency between input and result should feel like zero.
4. **Precision over decoration** — Use motion sparingly. Animate state transitions (step entry, scale regeneration), not ambient decoration. Easing should be precise (ease-out), never bouncy.
5. **The export is the product** — Every step exists to produce a better artifact. The export step is the payoff; the onboarding is setup. Design flow should build toward that moment of completion.
